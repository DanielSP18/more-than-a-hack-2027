# Tiki-Data technical document

More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1: ball possession. Barcelona (H) v Athletic Club (A).

Repository: https://github.com/DanielSP18/more-than-a-hack-2027

Submission: `out/Tiki-Data.csv`, regenerated by `python src/glue.py`
(sha256 `cdc8f16fdc7d6e896765c342275c8dd25e35fa9413731d07050e1293a315db54`).

## 1. The approach in one paragraph

Three independent pieces each read the provided tracking (and, for one of them, the Smart Tagging)
and write per-frame *evidence*: a probability for H, A and D on every frame, or "no idea". A glue
script multiplies the evidence (a weighted product of experts) and a hidden Markov model with
minimum durations decodes it into one label per frame with Viterbi, each half on its own. Nothing
is learned and no labelled data is used. Every constant comes from football reasoning or from
label-free measurements of this match's tracking, and the reason is written next to where it is
defined.

The approach numbers come from our original team plan. Approaches 1, 3 and 4 produce evidence,
approach 2 is the HMM that decodes it (§3.6), and approach 5, our own ball detection from the
video, was never built.

```
 tracking (ATD) ─┬─► approach 1: who controls the ball          ─┐
                 ├─► approach 3: dead balls + restarts  ◄─ tags ─┼─► product of experts ─► approach 2: HMM ─► Tiki-Data.csv
                 └─► approach 4: team shape (who, when no ball) ─┘      (src/glue.py)        (src/hmm.py)
```

## 2. Why this design

- spellF1 scores whole possessions, matched by temporal IoU, so a single flickering frame creates
  a spurious possession and costs as much as a whole missed one. A decoder
  with minimum durations and an explicit cost per change of possession addresses that directly. A
  sliding majority vote (the baseline) only blurs it.
- The three kinds of evidence cover different frames. Ball control speaks when a player has the
  ball, dead-ball detection when nobody does, and team shape on the 41% of graded frames where the
  ball is not tracked. Because each one writes probabilities with an explicit "no idea", the
  decoder can weigh them without hand-written precedence rules.
- Training on the provided game is forbidden, and we did not obtain labelled data from other games
  in time (see §4), so a learned model was out. We built a rule-based system instead and wrote down
  the reason for every constant.

## 3. The pieces

### 3.1 Shared frame table (`src/load.py`)

We parse the tactical ATD once into a cached table indexed by video frame (ATD `frame_id` − 1), in
metres on a 105.3 × 68 m pitch: ball position, ball speed (centred difference, because the ATD's own
ball-speed field is unusable), players, team of each track slot, distance to the nearest H and A
player, and the graded half of each frame. Player speed is in m/s (checked against positions: ratio
0.99).

### 3.2 Approach 1: ball control (`src/approach1.py`)

A frame is controlled by a team when the nearest player is within 1.5 m of the ball, the ball is
slower than 12 m/s, and no opponent is also within 1.5 m.
- 1.5 m: with a slow ball the nearest-player distance peaks at 0.25 to 0.75 m and has mostly
  tailed off by 1.5 m, which is a stride plus a touch. Tracking jitter (~0.6 cm/frame) is
  negligible next to it.
- 12 m/s: faster than a player can run with the ball. Above 15 m/s the ball is within reach of
  anyone only 6% of the time (passes and shots going past players).

Evidence: 0.8 for that team on a controlled frame; 0.8 for D on a frame where the ball is seen
more than a margin outside the pitch; "no idea" otherwise (no ball, ball in flight, contested).

Approach 1 also contains a complete rule-based decoder (touches, confirmation by a 0.4 s touch or
a completed pass, backdating the switch). It was our intermediate candidate; the final pipeline
uses only its evidence.

### 3.3 Approach 3: dead balls and restarts (`src/approach3.py`)

1. Clean the tags by rule. A tagged goal is trusted only if the tracking shows the ball kicked
   from the centre spot at the following kick-off. On this match that rejects one tagged "goal"
   (around 31'), which the tracking shows to be a long stoppage (the cooling break), so it is kept
   as a stoppage. No frame number is named in the code.
2. Find the exact restart frame inside each 35 s tag window: the ball kicked or thrown from where
   that restart is taken (touchline, corner arc, goal area, centre spot), after a second with no
   live play. Of the 58 tagged restarts, 39 are found at the spot, 17 by the first moving ball, and
   2 fall back to the tag time.
3. Decide who restarts: the tag's team when it names one; the team that conceded after a goal;
   otherwise the nearest player at the restart, cross-checked for throw-ins against the last
   toucher before the ball went out.
4. Mark dead-ball stretches. Strong D (0.85) runs from the last frame the ball was seen moving in
   play to the restart, capped by how long each kind of stoppage can last (throw-in 20 s, goal kick
   30 s, corner 40 s, free kick 60 s). Weak D (0.65) covers the ball missing for ≥ 8 s while the
   median player speed is below 1.0 m/s, which catches fouls, offsides and injuries where play
   stops without the ball going out. Both conditions are required: the tracker loses the ball 41%
   of the time, and players are also slow during a slow build-up. The restarting team gets 0.8 for
   the first 2 s after each restart.
5. Find stoppages the tags missed. The Smart Tagging holds 59 set pieces for the match, including
   only 5 free kicks, 26 throw-ins and 10 goal kicks, where a LaLiga match has ~110 to 130
   stoppages. So the tracking looks for the ball going out where no tag accounts for it: the ball
   seen outside the pitch for 3+ frames, or lost within 2 m of a line for 3 s+. The restart is the
   next live frame at least 2 s later (the quickest throw-in) with a quiet second before it.
   Earlier moving balls are the ball bouncing back off the boards or being tossed to the taker.
   D is strong when the ball was seen out and play restarts from that line's spot (a throw-in from
   the touchline, a goal kick or corner from the goal line). It is weak when the ball was seen out
   but play resumes elsewhere, or when it was only lost at the line and play restarted from the
   spot. A ball lost at a line with play resuming elsewhere is not claimed. This adds 17 stoppages
   (median 7.7 s seen out, 8.8 s lost at the line, against 11.3 s for tagged throw-ins).

### 3.4 Approach 4: team shape (`src/approach4.py`)

A team with the ball spreads out to open passing lanes; a team without it compacts. Per frame we
take the Barcelona-minus-Athletic length and width of the outfield players (10th to 90th
percentile, so one straggler does not count), smooth over ±1 s, scale each by its typical size,
average, and squash with tanh to a probability for Barcelona between 0.27 and 0.73, which keeps the
signal weak on purpose. D is left at 1/3 because shape says nothing about dead balls. There is no
opinion when fewer than 6 outfield players of a team are visible.

We checked each feature against a label-free proxy, the team of the player within 1 m of the ball,
only to see which way it points: length agrees 73.6%, width 78.4%, the combined score 79.5%.

### 3.5 Combining (`src/glue.py`)

Weighted product of experts: per frame, Σ weight × log(max(p, 0.05)), renormalised. Weights:
approach 1 = 1, approach 3 = 1, approach 4 = 1/3.
- 1 and 3 are equal because each already calibrates its own confidence and they mostly speak on
  different frames.
- Team shape is averaged over ±1 s, so it cannot place a change more finely than a 2 s window. At
  1/3, its strongest opinion needs ~2.5 s of steady disagreement to pay for a change of possession
  by itself. That is longer than its window, so it can tip stretches where the ball is missing but
  cannot outvote the ball. The masked check in §7 shows that it helps, and that 2/3 instead of 1/3
  would change nothing beyond noise.

### 3.6 Approach 2: decoding (`src/hmm.py`)

States H, A, D, decoded per half (nothing carried across halftime; frames outside the halves are X).
- Minimum durations come from chains of sub-states. A possession lasts ≥ 1 s: a first touch alone
  takes 0.3 to 0.5 s, so anything shorter is a touch, deflection or block, which is not a change by
  the challenge's definition. A stoppage lasts ≥ 2 s (the quickest throw-in).
- Transitions use typical top-flight figures, not figures from this match: ~60 live minutes in
  ~200 possessions (mean 18 s), ~45 dead minutes in ~130 stoppages (mean 20 s). Half of possessions
  end with the ball going dead and half by a turnover in play. A restart can go to either team and
  the evidence decides which. Both hand-overs, H→A and H→D→A, can be produced.
- Emissions are (1/3) × log p, with p floored at 0.05. Consecutive frames are not independent
  observations, and the 1/3 is calibrated so that approach 1's own confirmation rule (10 frames of
  control at 0.8) just pays for one change of possession.
- There is no initial-state prior. The first half is graded from a live kick-off and the second
  from ~2 minutes before one, so the evidence decides how each half opens.

## 4. Assets used and dropped

| asset | used? | how |
|---|---|---|
| Tactical ATD (tracking) | yes | everything: ball, players, teams, speeds |
| Smart Tagging | yes | approach 3: set-piece and goal tags as restart windows, cleaned by rule. The 579 phase tags were tested as possession evidence and dropped (below) |
| Homography | no | the ATD is already in pitch coordinates; we never needed the video |
| Tactical / broadcast video | no | our own detection or ball tracking was planned (approach 5) but not built |
| Broadcast ATD | no | it is on its own timeline, with no mapping to the tactical video |

Tried and dropped:
- Majority-vote decoder (`src/decode.py`, used by `src/v0.py`): replaced by the HMM, which has
  explicit minimum durations instead of a blur.
- Approach 1's own rule decoder: superseded by the HMM, which can weigh approaches 3 and 4 as well.
- Back-line height as a team-shape feature: it points the wrong way on the proxy check (47.7%).
- Pressure on the most-pressed player: weaker than length and width (62.9%) and lowered the
  combined agreement, so it is switched off.
- Short ball-gap interpolation (≤ 0.5 s): ball gaps here are long (median 10 to 12 s), so it fills
  only 19 graded frames. Approach 4 still computes it but does not use it.
- Phase tags as possession evidence (`src/phases.py`). The phases come in mirrored pairs (Build Up
  for one team is Defending in Attacking Third for the other, on identical intervals), so per frame
  only the strongest tag per team counts. Each code is calibrated against approach 1's ball-control
  frames: Build Up 87%, Progression 85%, Attacking Transition 73%, down to Players in the Box 59%
  (the Defending codes come out pointing at the other team, as they should). On the masked check
  (§7) they carry possession through hidden stretches (69.7% alone) but add nothing on top of team
  shape (72.7% vs 73.2%). They also make the decoder hold a team through turnovers: wrong
  deviations inside same-team ball gaps rise from 24% to 27 to 32%, and the possession count falls
  from 249 to between 208 and 237, depending on weight. A 20 to 30 s phase is too coarse to say
  *when* possession changed, so phase tags are not in the glue.
- Forward movement of each team as a team-shape feature: it agrees with the team in control on only
  59% of frames, and in the 3 s after a turnover it still favours the team that lost the ball
  (42%), because players keep running the way they were going before they turn.
- A stoppage detector from player movement (speeds smoothed over ±5 s; it separates code-confirmed
  stoppages from ball-control frames with AUC 0.87). The quiet time it finds is already inside
  approach 3's dead stretches, with at most ~2 minutes outside them. The stoppages we still miss
  happen while players move normally, so it would mostly flag live play.
- Attacking Transition starts as change points: at 15 of 24 the decoder already switches to the
  tagged team within 5 s, and at 2 it already had it. That leaves 7, too few to act on from a tag
  that is right 73% of the time.
- A model trained on other matches (planned with Metrica's open sample games): not done in time.

## 5. Training data

None. We used no labelled data of any kind, from this game or any other, and nobody labelled or
corrected a frame of the provided game by hand. The public 5-minute ground truth was used only to
check the pipeline end to end (file format, frame alignment, labels meaning what we think). It was
never used to choose a constant or a weight; each one is set by the reasoning written next to it.
On 17 public possessions the differences between the variants we ran were all within noise anyway.

## 6. Possession rules implemented

- Control: nearest player ≤ 1.5 m, ball < 12 m/s, no opponent equally close.
- Loose ball, contested ball or no ball: no evidence, so possession stays with the last team
  (sticky possession, as the challenge defines it).
- Touches without control (deflections, blocks, headers, aerial duels): a few frames of opponent
  evidence do not pay for a change; about 0.4 s of steady control does. A possession shorter than
  1 s cannot be produced.
- Ball out of the pitch: D from where play stopped to the restart frame.
- Stoppages without the ball going out (fouls, offsides, injuries): a long ball absence with slow
  players gives weak D.
- Restarts: the ball is live from the frame it moves faster than 3 m/s from the restart spot, and
  the restarting team is favoured for the first 2 s.
- Goals: confirmed by a centre-spot kick-off; the conceding team restarts.
- Dead-ball ownership: not modelled. A dead ball is always D, as the challenge asks.

## 7. Results we can see

On the full match the submission has 249 possessions (mean 16.1 s) and labels graded frames
H 38.6%, A 24.6%, D 36.8%. Ball-in-play time in LaLiga is typically 52 to 55 minutes of ~100,
which puts dead ball nearer 45 to 50% of graded frames, so we under-detect stoppages (see §8).
Wherever approach 3 says D, the final labels keep D 99.6% of the time, so the shortfall is in
detection, not in the decoder.

To check the pieces without labels (`analysis/masked_check.py`), we hide approach 1's ball-control
evidence on every third 30 s block of each half, which blinds a third of the match on purpose. We
decode the rest with the HMM and ask how often the decoded team on the hidden frames is the team
approach 1 had seen in control there. Ball control is computed by code, so this uses no labels. It
measures how well the other pieces carry possession through a stretch with no ball.

| pieces | team correct on hidden frames | wrongly called dead |
|---|---|---|
| ball control + dead balls | 38.2% | 39.5% |
| + team shape (the submission) | **73.2%** | 11.6% |
| + phase tags instead | 69.7% | 11.9% |
| + both | 72.7% | 10.8% |

Without a team signal the decoder holds the last state through a blind stretch, which after a
stoppage means holding D. Team shape fixes most of that.

Public 5-minute window, for pipeline checking only (17 possessions; with 1000 bootstrap draws,
spellF1 gaps below ~0.40 are noise):

| | macroF1 | spellF1 |
|---|---|---|
| published baseline (`baseline_smooth50`) | 0.621 | 0.326 |
| approach 1 alone, own rule decoder | 0.615 | 0.377 |
| pipeline before step 5 of approach 3 | 0.759 | 0.507 |
| **Tiki-Data (final)** | **0.746** | **0.490** |

The final file scores slightly lower here than the version without approach 3's step 5. The whole
difference is 50 frames, exactly the 2 s of restarting-team evidence, at a single untagged restart
in this window that we place early. One event in 5 minutes is not evidence either way, and
choosing between the two versions on it would be the tuning the rules forbid. Step 5 stays because
it recovers 17 stoppages across the full match, on reasoning and label-free checks alone.

## 8. Limitations and what we would do next

- There is no validation set. Constants come from reasoning and label-free statistics, and we never
  measured the full system against labelled possession data. Our first next step would be to label
  possessions on Metrica's open sample games and fit the transition rates, the emission weight and
  the piece weights there.
- The ball is missing on 41% of graded frames. When it reappears with the other team, we know
  possession changed but not when. The HMM places the switch where the evidence balances, which is
  often seconds off. Our own ball detection on the tactical video (approach 5) would address this.
- Stoppages are under-detected, and this is our largest error. We label ~37% of graded frames dead
  against an expected 45 to 50%. The tags miss about half the stoppages, and fouls are the hardest
  to recover from tracking: play stops with the ball visible at someone's feet, with no ball-out
  and no tag. Detecting them relies on "ball missing + players slow", which misses quick free kicks
  and can fire on a slow build-up with a lost ball. Next we would try a stationary ball with
  players walking away from it, or the referee's whistle where the video carries audio.
- Untagged restart timing is less certain than tagged: a ball tossed to the thrower at the
  touchline passes the same test as the throw itself.
- Frames are treated as independent apart from one global down-weighting. A semi-Markov model with
  explicit duration distributions would be more faithful.
- Smart Tagging errors propagate into approach 3 when they pass the rule checks.
- One number is match specific. Approach 4 gives no team-shape opinion before the second-half
  kick-off, using the frame published in the organisers' FAQ (113742) rather than detecting it.
  The number comes from the organisers, not from watching the video. A version for another match
  would detect the kick-off the way approach 3 does.

## 9. Reproduction

Requirements: Python 3.11, and the data bundle unpacked into `data/` as described in
`data/README.md` (only `data/tactical/` is read).

```bash
git clone https://github.com/DanielSP18/more-than-a-hack-2027.git
cd more-than-a-hack-2027
python3.11 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
# unpack the data bundle into data/
python src/glue.py
```

`src/glue.py` builds the frame table, runs approaches 1, 3 and 4, combines and decodes them, writes
`out/Tiki-Data.csv`, validates it with the official validator, and prints its sha256, which should
be `cdc8f16fdc7d6e896765c342275c8dd25e35fa9413731d07050e1293a315db54`. The pipeline is
deterministic and runs in under a minute on a laptop.
